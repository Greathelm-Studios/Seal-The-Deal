Market research done on 2026-11-15 for **Seal the Deal** (2v2 to 4v4 friend-group PvP on Steam, Early Access). Sales figures are third-party estimates unless a developer announced them. Re-check anything older than six months before quoting it in a pitch.

## Decisions Adam made from this (2026-11-15)

- Price: **$7.99 US, £7.99 UK** (recorded in `docs/GDD.md`).
- The team is 5–6 people and is bootstrapped. The money target is **£18k–30k net in year one** (£3k–5k each).

## Base rate

- Median 2025 Steam release grossed about **$249**. 66% made under $1,000, 90% under $50,000, about 8% passed $100,000.
- 444 friend-group ("friendslop") games released Jan 2025 to Sep 2026. One report (Solarsuit, Oct 2026) finds one with strong wishlists is as risky as an average Steam game: **28% of them sell**. Only the summary was read; the PDF would not open.
- Top friend-group titles fade to about 10% of their peak audience within 3–9 months.
- Early Access runs a median of about 14 months before 1.0.

These are survivors. PEAK launched with 29,384 wishlists and a 4-day-old store page; R.E.P.O. converted 38% of wishlists by day 7. Others who copied Content Warning's free launch day did not see the same result.

## What a copy is worth

- Developers net **40–55% of US list price** after Steam's 30%, refunds and regional pricing. Used here: about **$4, or £3, a copy** (assumes about $1.30 to the pound).
- Median week-one sales are **10–15% of launch wishlists**. Year one is **2–5x week one**.
- £18k net is about 6,000 copies, which needs about **14,000 launch wishlists** in a typical case (8,000 to 30,000 across good and poor conversion).
- £30k net is about 10,000 copies, about **23,000 launch wishlists** (13,000 to 50,000).
- On the base rate that is roughly a 1 in 10 outcome. The agent's own estimate for this game was 15–30% for £18k; that is judgement, not data.
- All before tax and before any publisher share.

## How the hits were marketed

- The game is the marketing: people watch streamers play a clear, funny setup, then buy it for their own group ("audience format", GameDiscoverCo).
- Short clips drive wishlists. Dear Passengers passed 1M wishlists from one trailer with no ads and no paid creators. No evidence of paid ads working in this genre was found.
- A demo is the strongest sourced lever (Roadside Research: 903k demo players).
- Super Battle Golf's streamers came back 5–6 times in three weeks and ran their own tournaments.
- Oro said it signed Roadside Research on a concept pitch and was involved "from day 0".

## Buyers and refunds

- The US is the largest buyer: Roadside Research was 47% US, Germany 10%, Brazil 5%, UK 4%, Canada 4%. Super Battle Golf skews more US and Canada. PEAK was about 25% US, then Russia, UK, China.
- Across hit Steam games the US is about 21% of players and China 8–11%.
- Refunds: median 9.5% across about 150 developers; Early Access median 12.4%.
- China: developers report 15–28% refunds (two games: US 5.5% and 12.7%, China 15% and 24%), at prices 40–60% below USD. A 27% share of copies is about 16–18% of revenue. Without a Chinese translation China is about 1–2% of sales for text-heavy games; 10–22% with one.
- Steam refunds automatically under 2 hours played within 14 days.

## Not found

- Oro Interactive's deal terms or revenue split.
- Named flops with numbers, and any sales split between PvP and co-op friend-group games.
- A country split for PvP friend-group games as a group.
- Whether a missing Chinese translation draws negative reviews.
- Steam's suggested pound price for a $7.99 game.

## How to apply

- Judge marketing by **wishlists per week** against the 14,000–23,000 target, not by views.
- Aim clips, streamers and playtests at US evening hours.
- Plan for 10–12% refunds.
- Do not quote the sales figures above as facts in a pitch without re-checking the source.
